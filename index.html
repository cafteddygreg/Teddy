BitSure

An automated trading platform. It began as a Telegram bot and is turning into a full web app.

Code on GitHub

At a glance

The problem
Testing a trading idea by hand takes time, and testing it with real money is risky.
What I built
A Telegram bot and web app with paper trading, backtesting, alerts and a score of my own.
What's next
Fix the production errors, then bring every bot feature to the website.

Why I built it

I wanted one place for market analysis, alerts and testing trading ideas without risking money. The Telegram bot came first. Now every feature of the bot is moving to the website, and the site should stay useful for people who don't pay.

What's inside

Dashboard
Paper equity, profit and loss, win rate and the Teddy Score in one view.
Markets
A live ticker for BTC, ETH, SOL and XRP, with charts from 5 minutes to 1 day.
Alerts and watchlist
Follow the pairs you care about and get notified.
Auto-trade and safety
Automatic trading with safety rules around it.
Strategy Lab
Test and compare rules on historical data. It is isolated from live trading and places no live orders.
Optimizer Lab
Search for better settings for a strategy.
French and English
Switch the interface language from the top bar.
Admin area
Tools to manage the platform, accounts and subscriptions.

Tujane

Direct and friendly urban carpooling in Bujumbura. One account to drive or to find a place.

Open the app

At a glance

The problem
Long bus queues and fuel shortages in Bujumbura.
What I built
A live carpooling app with Google sign-in, no commission and a 4-digit boarding code.
What's next
French and Kirundi versions.

Why I built it

I take the bus every day and see the same things: long queues, crowds, no shelter from the sun. Fuel shortages make it worse, and public transport can't carry everyone.

Tujane lets people who are already making a trip share it, and share the fuel cost.

Three promises

30 daysFree trial with every new account.
0 commissionDrivers and riders share fuel costs directly and fairly.
4 digitsA secret code checked at boarding, so you know you got in the right car.

These are the commitments written on the app's welcome screen.

How a trip works

An illustration, not live data.

Built with

  • FastAPI
  • SQLite, PostgreSQL later
  • PWA
  • Google sign-in
  • Railway

Where it stands

The app is online and an early build. The interface is in English today. French and Kirundi are planned, because it has to work for people in Bujumbura.

Igitoro Live

Fuel station status for Bujumbura. Check which station is serving before you drive there.

At a glance

The problem
People waste fuel driving between stations to find one that is serving.
What I built
A five-state status board for fuel stations, shown on this page as a demo.
What's next
Real station data and a first public version.

Why I built it

When fuel is short, people drive from station to station to find out which one is serving. That wastes fuel they may not have.

Igitoro Live shows the situation at each station. It is also the project I would show first to local organisations, as an example of what I can build for them.

Five states

  • Serving
  • Short queue
  • Medium queue
  • Blocked
  • Dry

Try it

A demo made for this page with made-up stations. Tap one to move it through the five states.

Where it stands

An early build, so there are no screenshots yet. They will go here when the app is ready to show.

How I build

I start from a problem I have seen myself, write down what the app has to do, build it in small pieces, test each piece and fix what breaks. When something fails in production, I read the logs, find the cause and ship the fix.

  1. IdeaStart from a problem I can see.
  2. SpecWrite down what it should do.
  3. BuildSplit it into small pieces and make each one.
  4. TestRun the app and the automated tests.
  5. DebugRead the logs and find the real cause.
  6. ShipDeploy it and watch what happens.
